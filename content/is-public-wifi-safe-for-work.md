## The short answer

Public Wi-Fi is not as safe as your home network, but it is not automatically dangerous either. The main risk is that you do not control the network, so you cannot be sure who else is on it or whether it is set up correctly. If you use a VPN, keep two-factor authentication turned on, and avoid logging in to sensitive accounts on networks you do not trust, you can work from a cafe or a co-working space safely. The steps in this article take about ten minutes to set up once, and then they protect you every time you log on.

This article is based on published information, not a hands-on review. We have not tested these products ourselves.

## Why public Wi-Fi is riskier

At home, you control your router, your password and who can join your network. On public Wi-Fi, none of that is true. Anyone in the same cafe can join the same network. Some networks are open, which means they have no password at all. Others use a shared password that everyone in the building knows. In both cases, your traffic is closer to strangers than it is at home.

Most modern websites use HTTPS, which encrypts the connection between your browser and the website. That is good news. It means someone on the same network cannot easily read your emails or your bank details just by watching the traffic. But HTTPS does not protect everything. It does not hide which websites you visit, and it does not protect apps that do not use encryption. A fake Wi-Fi network is another risk: someone can set up a network with the same name as a real cafe network, and your device may join it without you noticing.

## The risks, in plain English

| Risk | What it means | How serious it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Someone watching your traffic | Another person on the same network sees what you send | Lower on HTTPS sites, higher on old apps |
| Fake Wi-Fi network | A network with a trusted name that is not real | High, because you may not notice |
| Stolen login | Someone captures a password or a session | High, if you have no two-factor authentication |
| Malware on the network | A compromised network serves a bad download | Medium, keep your software updated |
| Shoulder surfing | Someone reads your screen in a busy cafe | Medium, use a privacy screen |

## What to do before you log on

### 1. Turn on your VPN first

Connect to your VPN before you open email, Slack or any work tool. A VPN encrypts all of your traffic, not just your browser, so it protects apps as well as websites. If your VPN has a kill switch, turn it on. The kill switch blocks your internet if the VPN drops, so your traffic is never sent without protection.

### 2. Check the network name

Ask a member of staff for the exact name of the Wi-Fi network. Do not join a network just because it has the cafe name in it. If you see two networks with similar names, ask which one is real. If your device joins the wrong one automatically, forget the network and join the right one.

### 3. Turn off automatic Wi-Fi joining

Your phone and laptop may join known networks automatically. That is convenient, but it can also join a fake network with a familiar name. Turn off auto-join for public networks, and only join networks you choose yourself.

### 4. Use two-factor authentication

Turn on two-factor authentication for your email, your bank, your password manager and your work tools. Even if someone steals your password, they still need the second factor, which is usually a code on your phone. This is the single most effective step on this list.

### 5. Keep your software updated

Install updates for your operating system, your browser and your apps. Updates often fix security problems that attackers use. If you are about to work in public, check for updates at home first, where the connection is safer.

## What to avoid on public Wi-Fi

- Do not log in to your bank or to sensitive work systems unless you have to. If you must, use a VPN and two-factor authentication.
- Do not download files from a network you do not trust, and do not accept software updates that appear while you are on public Wi-Fi. Wait until you are home.
- Do not enter card details on a website that does not show a padlock in the address bar. If the padlock is missing, the connection is not encrypted.
- Do not leave your laptop unlocked when you walk away, even for a minute. Lock the screen every time.

## What about your phone hotspot?

Your own phone hotspot is safer than public Wi-Fi, because you control the password and you know who is connected. If you have mobile data to spare, use your hotspot instead of a cafe network. It is not free, and it uses your data allowance, but it is the simplest safe option when you need to work in public and do not have a VPN ready.

## A simple routine for working from a cafe

1. Ask for the real network name and join it yourself.
2. Turn on your VPN and check that it is connected.
3. Open your work tools and check that two-factor authentication is working.
4. Work as normal, and lock your screen whenever you leave your seat.
5. When you leave, forget the network so your device does not rejoin it later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is public Wi-Fi safe if I only visit HTTPS websites?

Safer, but not fully safe. HTTPS encrypts the content of your connection, but it does not hide which websites you visit, and it does not protect apps that do not use encryption. A VPN adds protection for everything, not just your browser.

### What is the biggest mistake people make on public Wi-Fi?

Joining a network without checking the name. Fake networks with a trusted name are common in busy places. Always ask staff for the exact name, and turn off auto-join for public networks.

### Is it safe to work from a co-working space?

A co-working space is safer than an open cafe network if it has a private, password-protected network for members. Even then, you share the network with many people you do not know, so use a VPN and two-factor authentication in the same way.
