## The short answer

For most freelancers, 1Password is the better choice. It has stronger sharing controls, better support for clients and contractors, and a more complete set of features. NordPass is a good cheaper alternative, especially if you already use NordVPN, because the two work well together and the price is lower on long-term plans. If you work alone and only need a safe place for your own logins, either one will do the job. If you share logins with clients, a small team, or a virtual assistant, 1Password is worth the extra cost.

This article is based on published information, not a hands-on review. We have not tested these products ourselves.

## 1Password

1Password is built around vaults. You have a personal vault for your own logins, and you can create shared vaults for clients, projects or a small team. Each vault has its own permissions, so you can give a client access to one project without showing them your other logins. That matters for freelancers, because you often work with several clients at once and you do not want them to see each other's accounts.

1Password also has a feature for sharing a single login with someone who does not use 1Password. You can send a secure link that expires after a set time, which is useful when a client needs temporary access to an account. The admin controls let you see who has access to what, and remove people when a project ends. 1Password has apps for Windows, Mac, iOS, Android and Linux, and browser extensions for the main browsers.

Pros: detailed sharing controls, shared vaults with permissions, secure guest sharing, strong admin controls.
Cons: no free plan, costs more than NordPass, some features take time to learn.

## NordPass

NordPass is made by the same company as NordVPN. If you already pay for NordVPN, NordPass is a natural addition, because you can use the same account and the two products share the same design. NordPass has shared folders for sharing logins with clients or a small team, secure password sharing, and an admin panel for managing users. It is simpler than 1Password, and that is both its strength and its weakness. It is easier to set up, but it has fewer controls for complex sharing.

NordPass has a free plan with limited features, which is useful if you want to try it before you pay. The paid plans are usually cheaper than 1Password on long-term plans, based on published prices. If your needs are simple, and you mainly need a safe place for your own logins plus occasional sharing, NordPass is enough.

Pros: simple to use, works well with NordVPN, cheaper long-term plans, free plan available.
Cons: fewer sharing controls than 1Password, guest sharing is more limited, fewer advanced features.

## Sharing with clients

Sharing is where the two products differ most. A freelancer often needs to share a login with a client for a few weeks, then take the access away. 1Password handles this well, because you can share a single item, set an expiry, and remove access at any time. You can also give a client access to one vault without giving them access to your personal vault.

NordPass can share logins too, through shared folders, but the controls are simpler. That is fine for a long-term client, but less flexible when you work with many short-term clients. If you juggle five clients at once, 1Password will save you time and mistakes. If you have one or two long-term clients, NordPass will be fine.

## Security

Both products use strong encryption, and both support two-factor authentication. Both will warn you about weak, reused or compromised passwords. A password manager only helps if you fix the weak passwords it finds.

## Price

Both products charge a monthly fee, with a discount on yearly plans. NordPass is usually cheaper, based on published prices, and it has a free plan. 1Password has no free plan, but it offers a trial. Before you buy, check the renewal price, because it is often higher than the first-term price. If you already pay for NordVPN, check whether a bundle with NordPass is cheaper than buying them separately.

## Which one should you buy?

- Choose 1Password if you share logins with several clients, need detailed permissions, or want the most complete feature set.
- Choose NordPass if you already use NordVPN, want a lower price, or need something simple for your own logins.
- Choose either one if you work alone and only need a safe place for your own passwords, because both cover that well.

## How to move to a password manager as a freelancer

1. Pick one tool and create an account.
2. Import your saved passwords from your browser, or add them one by one.
3. Turn on two-factor authentication for your password manager and for your email.
4. Create a separate vault or folder for each client, so their logins never mix.
5. Share a login with a client through the password manager, never by email or chat.
6. Remove a client from a shared vault as soon as the project ends, and change the shared passwords if needed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to share passwords with clients through a password manager?

Yes, sharing through a password manager is safer than sharing in email or chat. The password is encrypted, you can set an expiry, and you can remove access at any time.

### Do I need a business plan as a freelancer?

A business or team plan is useful if you share with clients or contractors, because it gives you admin controls and the ability to remove people. If you work alone and never share, a personal plan is enough.

### What happens if I forget my master password?

Your master password is the one password you must remember, because it unlocks your vault. Both 1Password and NordPass offer account recovery options, but they are limited by design. Write your master password down on paper and store it somewhere safe, separate from your devices.
