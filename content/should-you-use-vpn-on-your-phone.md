## The short answer

Yes, if you work from your phone on public Wi-Fi, a VPN is worth using. Your phone is not a safer device than your laptop — it carries your email, your work chats, your files, and often your bank app, and it connects to more networks you do not control. A VPN on your phone encrypts that traffic in the same way it does on a laptop. If you only use your phone on your home Wi-Fi or on mobile data, a VPN matters less, but it still hides your browsing from your internet provider and from the network owner. The cost is small: most VPNs include phone apps in the same subscription as your laptop, so you do not need to pay twice.

## Why your phone is at risk too

Many people think of their phone as a safe device, because it is personal and always with them. In practice, a phone is often less safe than a laptop. It connects to Wi-Fi networks automatically, it joins networks in cafes, airports, hotels and co-working spaces without asking, and it runs dozens of apps that send data in the background. Some of those apps do not use encryption, which means their traffic can be read on an open network.

Your phone also holds more of your life than your laptop does. Your email, your messages, your photos, your banking app, and your work tools are all in one place. If someone gets access to your phone's traffic on a public network, they can learn a lot about you very quickly.

## When a VPN on your phone helps

| Situation | Does a VPN help? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Working from a cafe on public Wi-Fi | Yes, a lot | You do not control the network |
| Working from an airport or hotel | Yes, a lot | Busy networks with many strangers |
| Working on mobile data (4G/5G) | A little | Mobile data is safer than public Wi-Fi, but a VPN still hides your traffic from your carrier |
| Working from home Wi-Fi | A little | Your home network is safer, but a VPN hides your browsing from your internet provider |
| Using a work app with its own encryption | A little | The app is already encrypted, but a VPN protects your other apps too |
| Streaming or browsing at home | Up to you | A VPN can help with privacy, but it is not essential |

## What a VPN on your phone does not do

A VPN is not a complete security tool. It encrypts your internet traffic, but it does not protect you from everything. It does not stop you from downloading a malicious app, it does not stop phishing emails, and it does not protect you if someone steals your phone and it is unlocked. You still need a screen lock, two-factor authentication, and regular software updates.

A VPN also does not make you anonymous. Your VPN provider can see your traffic, which is why choosing a provider with a clear privacy policy matters. And some apps, such as your bank app, may not work well over a VPN, or may ask you to verify your identity more often, because your location appears to change.

## How to set up a VPN on your phone

Most paid VPNs have an app for iOS and Android, and setting it up takes about five minutes.

1. Install the VPN app from your phone's official app store. Do not install a VPN from a link in an email or from a third-party website.
2. Log in with the same account you use on your laptop. Most VPN subscriptions cover several devices, so check the provider website for your device limit.
3. Turn on the kill switch if the app has one. On a phone, the kill switch blocks your internet if the VPN drops, so your traffic is never sent without protection.
4. Choose the WireGuard protocol if the app offers it. WireGuard is designed to handle the way phones switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data, and it reconnects faster than older protocols.
5. Turn on auto-connect for untrusted Wi-Fi networks, if the app offers it. That way the VPN starts by itself when you join a cafe network, and you do not have to remember.

After setup, test it once: connect to the VPN, open your browser, and check that your location has changed. If it has, the VPN is working.

## Battery and speed: the honest answer

The two most common worries about a VPN on a phone are battery and speed, and both are smaller problems than people expect. A modern VPN protocol such as WireGuard uses very little battery, because it is designed to be lightweight. Older protocols use more, so if your battery drops noticeably, check which protocol your app is using and switch to WireGuard if it is available.

Speed is similar. A VPN always adds some overhead, because your traffic is encrypted and routed through another server. On a fast connection, you may not notice it. On a slow public Wi-Fi network, the VPN can make things slower, because the network itself is already slow. If that happens, try a server closer to where you are. A nearby server is usually faster than one on the other side of the world.

## Free VPN apps: be careful

There are many free VPN apps in the app stores, and most of them are a bad idea for work. A free VPN has to pay for its servers somehow, and some free apps make money by showing ads, by limiting your data, or by collecting and selling information about what you do. That is the opposite of what a VPN is for.

If you already pay for a VPN on your laptop, use the same provider's phone app — it is included in your subscription. If you do not pay for a VPN, a free tier from a trusted provider, such as Proton VPN, is a safer choice than a free app from a company you have never heard of. Check the privacy policy before you install anything.

## A simple phone security checklist

- Use a screen lock — a PIN, a fingerprint, or face recognition — and set it to lock quickly.
- Turn on two-factor authentication for your email, your bank, and your work tools.
- Keep your phone's software updated, and update your apps regularly.
- Only install apps from the official app store.
- Turn off automatic Wi-Fi joining for networks you do not trust.
- Use a VPN when you work on public Wi-Fi.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a VPN drain my phone battery?

A little, but not much with a modern protocol. WireGuard is designed to be light on battery, and most people do not notice a difference. If your battery drops a lot after installing a VPN, check the protocol setting and switch to WireGuard, or check whether the app is running in the background when it does not need to be.

### Can I use the same VPN subscription on my phone and laptop?

Usually yes. Most paid VPNs cover several devices on one subscription — check the provider website for your exact limit. You log in with the same account on each device, and you do not need to pay twice.

### Should I leave the VPN on all the time?

You can, and many people do. Leaving it on means you are protected whenever you join a new network, without having to remember. The trade-off is a small amount of battery and speed. If you only work from your phone occasionally, turning it on when you join public Wi-Fi is enough.
