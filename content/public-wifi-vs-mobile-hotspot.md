## The short answer

Your phone hotspot is safer than public Wi-Fi, because you control the password and you know who is connected. Public Wi-Fi is shared with everyone in the building, and you cannot be sure who else is on it or whether the network itself is real. That does not mean public Wi-Fi is unusable — with a VPN and two-factor authentication, you can work from a cafe safely. But if you have mobile data to spare and you need to do something sensitive, such as logging in to your bank or sending a confidential file, your hotspot is the simpler and safer choice. Speed and cost are the trade-offs: a hotspot is usually slower than good cafe Wi-Fi, and it uses your data allowance.

## Why public Wi-Fi is riskier

A public Wi-Fi network is a shared space. When you join it, your device is on the same network as every other person in the cafe, including people you cannot see and do not know. Most modern websites use HTTPS, which encrypts your connection to the website, so a stranger cannot easily read your emails. But HTTPS does not protect everything. It does not hide which websites you visit, and it does not protect apps that do not use encryption.

The bigger risk is a fake network. Anyone can set up a Wi-Fi network with the same name as a real cafe network, and your phone or laptop may join it automatically. Once you are on a fake network, the person running it can see your traffic, redirect you to fake login pages, or serve you malicious downloads. The only defence is to ask staff for the exact network name, and to turn off automatic joining for public networks.

## Why a hotspot is safer

A mobile hotspot is a Wi-Fi network created by your phone, using your mobile data connection. You set the password, and only people you give the password to can join. There is no stranger on the network, and there is no fake network to join by mistake, because the network is yours.

Mobile data is also harder to intercept than Wi-Fi. Your phone's connection to the mobile network is encrypted by the carrier, and it does not pass through a router owned by a stranger. That does not make it perfectly safe — no connection is — but it removes the two biggest risks of public Wi-Fi: unknown people on the same network, and fake networks.

## Speed: the honest comparison

Speed is where public Wi-Fi usually wins. A good cafe or co-working space has a fast broadband connection, and if few people are using it, it can be faster than your phone's mobile data. A hotspot depends on your mobile signal. In a city centre with strong 4G or 5G, a hotspot can be fast enough for video calls. In a basement, a rural area, or a crowded place where many people are using the same mobile network, it can be slow.

If you need to make video calls, test both. Join the cafe Wi-Fi and try a call. If it is laggy or drops, switch to your hotspot and try again. The faster option changes from place to place, so there is no single right answer. What matters is that you test before an important call, not during it.

## Cost: data is not free

A hotspot uses your mobile data allowance. How much it uses depends on what you do. Email and browsing use very little. Video calls use much more, and a long video call can use a large part of a monthly allowance. Downloading large files or watching video over a hotspot can use it up in an afternoon.

Check your data allowance before you rely on a hotspot for a full working day. If your plan has a small allowance, save the hotspot for sensitive work, and use public Wi-Fi — with a VPN — for the rest. If your plan has a large allowance, or unlimited data, the cost matters less, but check the provider's fair use policy, because some unlimited plans slow you down after a certain amount of hotspot data.

## When to use which

**Use your hotspot when:**
- You need to log in to your bank or to a sensitive work system.
- You are sending a confidential file or signing a contract.
- The public Wi-Fi looks suspicious, or there are two networks with similar names.
- You are in a busy place and you do not know who else is on the network.

**Use public Wi-Fi when:**
- You have a lot of data-heavy work to do and your mobile allowance is small.
- You need the fastest possible connection for a video call, and the cafe Wi-Fi is fast.
- You are doing low-risk work, such as reading articles or writing, and you have a VPN and two-factor authentication turned on.

**Use both, with a VPN, when:**
- You want the best of both. A VPN encrypts your traffic on public Wi-Fi, and it also protects you on a hotspot. If you already pay for a VPN, use it on both connections.

## How to set up a hotspot safely

1. Set a strong password for your hotspot. Do not use the default password, and do not use a simple one such as "12345678". A long password with letters and numbers is enough.
2. Turn the hotspot off when you are not using it. An open hotspot that nobody is using is still a network someone can try to join.
3. Check who is connected. Most phones show a list of connected devices. If you see a device you do not recognise, change your password.
4. Keep your phone's software updated. Updates fix security problems that attackers use.
5. Use a VPN on your laptop as well, even on a hotspot. A hotspot is safer than public Wi-Fi, but a VPN adds another layer of protection, and it hides your traffic from your mobile carrier too.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a mobile hotspot completely safe?

No connection is completely safe, but a hotspot is safer than public Wi-Fi for most work. You control the password, you know who is connected, and there is no fake network to join by mistake. You should still use a VPN for sensitive work, keep your software updated, and use two-factor authentication on your accounts.

### Does using a hotspot drain my phone battery?

Yes, a hotspot uses more battery than normal phone use, because your phone is acting as a router. If you plan to work on a hotspot for several hours, keep your phone plugged in, or carry a power bank. A phone that runs out of battery in the middle of a call is a problem a VPN cannot fix.

### Can I use a hotspot for video calls?

Yes, if your signal is strong enough. A video call needs a stable connection more than a fast one. If your signal is weak, or if you are in a crowded place, the call may freeze or drop. Test the hotspot with a short call before an important meeting, and have public Wi-Fi — with a VPN — as a backup.
