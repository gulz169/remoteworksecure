## The short answer

Your router is the front door of your home office. If it is not secure, every device on your network is at risk, including your work laptop. The good news is that securing it takes about 30 minutes, and you only need to do it once. Change the admin password, update the firmware, turn on WPA3 or WPA2 encryption, and set a strong Wi-Fi password. Then turn on the guest network for visitors, and check the connected devices once a month. This article walks through each step in order.

This article is based on published information, not a hands-on review. We have not tested these products ourselves.

## Why your router matters

Every device in your home connects through your router: your laptop, your phone, your printer, and your smart TV. If someone gets into your router, they can see the devices on your network, change your settings, and in some cases redirect your traffic to fake websites. Most routers are shipped with a default admin password such as "admin" or "password", and many people never change it. That is the first thing to fix.

## Before you start: what you need

You need a laptop or phone connected to your home Wi-Fi, and the router itself. Most routers have a label on the bottom or the back with the admin address, the default username and the default password. The admin address is usually something like 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1. You will also need a pen and paper, or a password manager, to save the new passwords you create.

## Step 1: Change the admin password (5 minutes)

Log in to your router admin page. Type the admin address into your browser, and sign in with the default username and password from the label. Go to the administration or system settings, and change the admin password to a long, unique password. Save it in your password manager.

## Step 2: Update the firmware (10 minutes)

Firmware is the software that runs your router. Manufacturers release updates that fix security problems, and old firmware is a common way into a home network. In the admin page, look for Firmware Update or System Update. Check for an update, install it, and let the router restart. If your router has an option to update automatically, turn it on.

## Step 3: Turn on WPA3 or WPA2 encryption (5 minutes)

Go to the wireless or Wi-Fi settings. Look for the security or encryption setting, and make sure it is set to WPA3, or WPA2 if your router does not support WPA3. Do not use WEP or WPA, because both are old and easy to break. If your router offers a choice between WPA2 and WPA3, choose WPA3 if all of your devices support it. If an old device cannot connect with WPA3, use WPA2 instead.

## Step 4: Set a strong Wi-Fi password (5 minutes)

In the same Wi-Fi settings page, change the Wi-Fi password to a long, unique password. A good Wi-Fi password is at least 16 characters, and it should not be your name, your address, or a word from the dictionary. Save it in your password manager, and share it with your household through the password manager, not by writing it on a note stuck to the router. If you have separate 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz networks, use the same strong password for both, or set a different strong password for each.

## Step 5: Turn on the guest network (5 minutes)

Most modern routers have a guest network feature. It creates a separate Wi-Fi network for visitors, so they can use the internet without seeing your work laptop, your printer, or your shared files. Turn the guest network on, give it a different password from your main network, and use it for visitors, for smart home devices, and for any device you do not fully trust. This is a simple way to keep your work devices separate from everything else in your home.

## Comparison: the settings that matter

| Setting | What to choose | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Admin password | Long, unique, saved in a password manager | Controls the router itself |
| Firmware | Latest version, auto-update on | Fixes security problems |
| Wi-Fi encryption | WPA3, or WPA2 if WPA3 is not supported | Encrypts your wireless traffic |
| Wi-Fi password | At least 16 characters, unique | Stops strangers joining your network |
| Guest network | On, with a separate password | Keeps visitors away from work devices |
| Remote admin | Off | Stops anyone managing the router from the internet |

## Step 6: Turn off remote administration

Remote administration lets you manage your router from the internet. Unless you have a specific reason to use it, turn it off in the administration or remote access settings.

## Step 7: Check the connected devices

In the admin page, look for a list of connected devices, usually called Device List, Connected Devices, or DHCP Clients. Check the list once a month. You should recognise every device on it. If you see a device you do not recognise, change your Wi-Fi password, and check whether a neighbour or a visitor is using your network. If you see a device you no longer use, remove it from the list if your router allows it.

## A monthly routine

1. Log in to the router and check for a firmware update.
2. Check the connected devices list and look for anything unfamiliar.
3. Change the Wi-Fi password if you have shared it with someone who no longer needs it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I update my router firmware?

Check once a month. If your router supports automatic updates, turn them on, and check once a month that the updates are actually installing. If your router is more than five years old and no longer gets updates, consider replacing it.

### Is WPA2 enough, or do I need WPA3?

WPA2 is still safe if you use a strong password, but WPA3 is better. If your router and your devices support WPA3, use it. If an old device cannot connect with WPA3, use WPA2, and replace the old device when you can.

### What should I do if I forget the admin password?

You will need to reset the router to factory settings, usually by holding the reset button on the back for about 10 seconds. That will erase all of your settings, including your Wi-Fi password, so you will need to set the router up again. This is why you should save the admin password in a password manager.
