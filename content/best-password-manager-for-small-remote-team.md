## The short answer

For most small remote teams, 1Password is the safest default. It has shared vaults, admin controls and apps for every device. NordPass is a good cheaper alternative, especially if your team already uses NordVPN. Bitwarden is the best free option for a very small team. Keeper is best if you need detailed admin reporting.

This article is based on published information, not a hands-on review. We have not tested these products ourselves.

## 1Password

1Password is the most complete option for a remote team. Each team member gets a personal vault, and shared vaults hold logins for tools the whole team uses. An admin can add or remove people, see who has access to what, and require two-factor authentication.

Pros: shared vaults, strong admin controls, apps for Windows, Mac, iOS and Android.
Cons: no free plan, costs more than Bitwarden.

## NordPass

NordPass is made by the same company as NordVPN. If your team already uses NordVPN, NordPass fits well. It has shared folders, secure password sharing and an admin panel for small teams.

Pros: simple to use, works well with NordVPN, cheaper long-term plans.
Cons: fewer advanced admin features than 1Password or Keeper.

## Keeper

Keeper is built for businesses that need control. It has detailed reports, role-based access and tools to check whether team passwords are weak or reused.

Pros: detailed reporting, strong admin controls.
Cons: interface is more complex, price is higher for small teams.

## Bitwarden

Bitwarden is open source and has a free plan for two users, with paid plans for larger teams. It has shared collections, secure sharing and apps for every device.

Pros: free plan, open source, low price.
Cons: interface is less polished, setup takes a little longer.

## How to choose a password manager for a team

Price is the last thing to check. These four questions decide whether a tool will actually work for your team.

**1. How does sharing work?** A team needs shared vaults or shared folders, not one person emailing passwords to everyone else. Check that an admin can create a shared vault, add people to it, and remove them in one place.

**2. What happens when someone leaves?** This is the test most teams forget. When a team member leaves, you must be able to remove them from the team account and from every shared vault immediately. A family plan usually cannot do this properly — that is the main reason to pay for a business plan.

**3. Can the admin enforce two-factor authentication?** A password manager is only as strong as its weakest account. The admin should be able to require two-factor authentication for every team member, and to see who has not turned it on yet.

**4. Will non-technical people actually use it?** The best password manager is the one your team opens every day. If the browser extension and phone app are awkward, people will go back to reusing passwords or writing them in a notebook. A free trial is the best way to find out — let two or three team members try it for a week before you pay for everyone.

## How to move your team to a password manager

1. Pick one tool and create a team account.
2. Invite your team and ask everyone to turn on two-factor authentication.
3. Move shared logins into a shared vault, not into chat or email.
4. Remove people from shared vaults as soon as they leave the team.
5. Check the admin report once a month for weak or reused passwords.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to share passwords in a password manager?

Sharing through a password manager is safer than sharing in email or chat. The password is encrypted, and you can remove access at any time.

### Do we need a business plan, or is a family plan enough?

A business plan is better for a team. It gives you an admin panel, the ability to remove people, and reports. A family plan does not have those controls.

### What happens when someone leaves the team?

Remove them from the team account and from shared vaults. Change the passwords for the most important shared accounts, such as email and banking.

### Should we use one shared login, or one login per person?

One login per person is safer. With a shared login, you cannot tell who logged in, and you cannot remove one person's access without changing the password for everyone. Give each person their own login, and share access to it through the password manager instead.

### Is a free password manager safe for a team?

A free plan from a reputable provider can be safe for a very small team, but check what is missing. Free plans often lack admin controls, detailed reports and priority support. For a team of more than two or three people, a paid business plan is usually worth it.
