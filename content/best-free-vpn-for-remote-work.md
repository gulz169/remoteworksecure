## Why free VPNs are limited

A VPN costs money to run. Servers, bandwidth and staff all have to be paid for. A paid VPN covers those costs from subscriptions. A free VPN has to cover them another way, and that is where the problems start. Some free VPNs limit how much data you can use, or how fast you can go, and hope you will upgrade to a paid plan. That is a fair model, and it is how the better free VPNs work. Others make money from advertising, from selling data about their users, or from showing you ads inside the app. That is a bad model for a privacy tool, because it defeats the purpose of using a VPN at all.

## What to check before you trust a free VPN

### Does it have a data limit?

Some free VPNs give you a small amount of data each month, for example enough for a few hours of browsing, but not enough for video calls or large downloads. If you work remotely, you will run out of data quickly. Proton VPN is unusual because its free tier has no data limit, based on its published free plan.

### Does it limit speed or servers?

Free tiers usually give you access to fewer servers, and those servers are shared by many free users, so they are slower. That matters for video calls, where a slow connection causes lag and dropped calls. If you need a VPN for work calls, a free tier will often be too slow.

### How does it make money?

Read the privacy policy. A good free VPN will say clearly that it does not sell your data, and it will explain that the free tier is paid for by the paid plans. If the privacy policy is vague, or if it says the provider may share data with advertisers, do not use that VPN for work. A VPN that sells your data is worse than no VPN at all, because it gives you a false sense of safety.

### Does it have a kill switch?

A kill switch blocks your internet if the VPN drops, so your real IP address is not exposed. Some free VPNs do not include a kill switch, or only include it on paid plans. If you work on public Wi-Fi, a kill switch matters, because a dropped connection without one leaves your traffic exposed.

## When a free VPN is enough

A free VPN is enough if you work remotely occasionally, you only need it for email and browsing, and you do not make video calls over it. It is also enough if you are trying a VPN for the first time and you want to see how it works before you pay. In those cases, Proton VPN is a good place to start.

## When you should pay for a VPN

You should pay for a VPN if you work remotely every day, you make video calls, you upload large files, or you work from public Wi-Fi regularly. A paid plan gives you the full server network, faster speeds, no data limit, and a kill switch. Most paid VPNs offer a 30-day money-back guarantee, so you can try one and get a refund if it does not work for you. That is effectively a free trial, without the limits of a free tier.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to use a free VPN for work?

It depends on the VPN. A free VPN from a trusted provider with a clear privacy policy, such as Proton VPN, is safe for light use. A free VPN from an unknown provider, with a vague privacy policy, is not safe for work. Check the privacy policy before you trust any free VPN.

### Why do free VPNs have data limits?

Because bandwidth costs money. A free VPN gives you a small amount of data to try the service, and hopes you will upgrade to a paid plan for more. Proton VPN is an exception, because its free tier has no data limit, based on its published plan.

### Can I use a free VPN for video calls?

You can try, but it will often be too slow. Free servers are shared by many users, so they are congested. If video calls are part of your work, a paid VPN is a better choice.
